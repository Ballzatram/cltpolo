# Charlotte Polo Club | Investment research and three-site planning brief

**Research date:** September 24, 2026  
**Release:** 2026-09-24-investment-r2  
**Decision status:** Feasibility research. Sites A and B are working public-record matches awaiting confirmation that the supplied Prospects token links resolve to these exact parcels; Site C remains open. No acquisition recommendation, verified local construction bids or demonstrated customer demand.

## Investment thesis

**Polo at the heart. Experience as the business.** A small core of players may create an authentic, differentiated experience, but the club must demonstrate enough contribution from carefully designed experiences to pay for year-round horses, paid staff, facilities and capital. A larger list of revenue streams is not evidence of a viable business. The present prototype does not close that gap.

This release maintains three independent property models. Site A is provisionally matched to 4252-1 Reid Rd in York County; Site B is provisionally matched to 1700 Westbrook Rd / the Lando tract in Chester County; Site C remains open. Because the supplied Prospects token pages could not be machine-read, the link-to-parcel identity must be confirmed before acquisition reliance. Costs, tax inputs, fencing, demand case, notes and diligence checks remain independent. Missing post-development property tax, verified flat acreage and drive time remain unknown, not zero. The deck does not rank unverified sites as investments.

The owner's `20 min` and `10 min` are interpreted as at least **20 total stalls** and **10 club-owned horses**. The prototype allocates two stalls to visitor/quarantine reserve, leaving eight potentially boardable stalls before occupancy. A 10-horse teaching string is not an unlimited supply of mounts for 20 concurrent players. Workload, rotation, individual suitability and turnout require professional approval.

The site brief remains 20+ parcel acres, flat contiguous usable polo footprint, arena first, a separately assessed grass format, the I-77 corridor south of Charlotte toward Columbia, and a documented drive of no more than about 45 minutes from Uptown Charlotte. Expansion is optional. Purchase price and extra acreage do not override location, terrain or permitted-use failures.

## Candidate property diligence added in release r2

### Site A — 4252-1 Reid Rd, Rock Hill, SC (working match)

The current public listing is approximately **32.8 acres at $725,000**, RUD-1, CMLS 4213472, using parcel/parent identifier 6130000014. The larger 58.84-acre parent tract is marketed as proposed tracts, with surveying described after a tract goes under contract. The final 32.8-acre legal boundary therefore remains a fundamental dependency for soil, stream, flood, access and tax analysis. [S20, S21]

The listing indicates a **well is required and no sewer is listed**. Piedmont groundwater is controlled largely by weathered material and bedrock fractures, so county-scale well averages are only context. A test well, sustained pump test and water-quality panel should be a feasibility condition if the property remains in consideration. Wastewater capacity for a horse facility plus public events must be established separately. [S20, S29–S31]

The parent tract marketing describes a flowing stream and small pond, while the individual tract listing does not claim waterfront. That is not necessarily contradictory because the proposed split controls which water features actually fall inside Site A. The final plat should be screened against current flood/wetland layers and field conditions. No parcel-specific SSURGO soil series or contiguous flat polo footprint has yet been verified. [S20, S21, S26]

York County's current code regulates commercial equestrian uses and the full polo-experience program requires a written classification covering instruction, boarding, ticketed events, corporate hospitality, lighting, food/alcohol and parking. Horse-friendly marketing language is not land-use approval. [S22]

### Site B — 1700 Westbrook Rd / Lando tract, Chester County, SC (working match)

The current public record/listing package identifies approximately **156 acres at $2,106,000**, TMS 133-00-00-033-000, zoning R-2. South Carolina Commerce lists **140 developable acres**, elevation roughly 420–500 feet and approximately 2.5 miles to I-77. “Developable” does not mean 140 flat polo acres. [S23, S34]

Utility access is materially better documented than Site A but still requires engineering. SC Commerce lists a **12-inch water line about 50 feet away** and an **18-inch force sewer about 1,600 feet away**. The sewer should therefore not be modeled as an on-site utility until the connection point, easements, capacity, pump/force-main requirements and extension costs are confirmed. [S23]

Fishing Creek borders roughly 4,000 feet of the property. Broker flood/topographic material shows a substantial 100-year floodplain/lowland corridor associated with the creek and interior drainage, while marketing material says uplands above the 100-year floodplain comprise roughly two-thirds of the tract. Permanent facilities and primary polo surfaces should be laid out on verified uplands unless engineering and permitting demonstrate otherwise. National Wetlands Inventory and broker maps are screening tools, not jurisdictional wetland determinations. [S23, S25, S26]

Chester County publishes a USDA-NRCS-derived SSURGO soil layer containing map-unit name, texture, depth to water table, flooding class, drainage class, hydrologic group, slope and hydric rating. The exact parcel AOI map units have **not yet been extracted into this model**; they should be overlaid with the concept plan and field-verified/geotechnically tested before arena, grass field, barn, parking and septic decisions. [S33]

A natural-gas transmission line crosses the property and a 30-foot deeded access easement serves the eastern portion. Those recorded easements and operator crossing/no-build requirements must be pulled before treating gross acreage as usable. Heavy industrial land use immediately west is a reason to prioritize a Phase I ESA; it is not evidence that contamination exists on the parcel. [S23]

R-2 permits equestrian uses in the current Chester County ordinance, but the broader experience-center program still needs a written classification for spectator events, hospitality, food/alcohol, lighting, sound and event frequency. [S24]

### Water-use framework for both South Carolina sites

South Carolina should **not** be modeled as though stream, pond or well access creates unlimited irrigation rights. Surface-water withdrawals above **3 million gallons in a month** enter the state withdrawal program. Outside designated Capacity Use Areas, high-volume groundwater systems above the applicable 3-million-gallon monthly threshold require registration; irrigation wells and potable business wells have separate permitting pathways. Peak-month arena and grass irrigation therefore needs to be quantified before choosing creek withdrawal, groundwater or public water. [S27–S29]

Current raw-land tax bills also should not be used as the operating pro forma. South Carolina rollback provisions can apply when qualifying agricultural use changes, and post-build improvements plus commercial/equestrian uses need assessor review. [S32]

## Demand: headcounts before population percentages

Using the owner's rounded one-million-person denominator for illustration, 0.5% is 5,000 people; 0.25% is 2,500; 500 is 0.05%; 250 is 0.025%; and 20 is 0.002%. The million-person figure is not used as a verified market-sizing input. Neither residents nor event attendance automatically become paying players.

Three predefined demand cases (definitions editable in the research data) hold the distinction between unique audience, repeat paid visits, paid players, lesson rider-sessions, corporate groups and private events. The same spectator returning four times creates four ticket sales, not four unique customers. Member-included mounts are reserved before selling new mounted sessions. Lesson counts are rider-sessions, not unique people. The proposed 250-person repeat audience and 20-player stretch case are owner-inspired hypotheses, not proven ceilings or signed demand.

## Building the facility without double counting

A barn shell, stall systems and a fully operating horse facility are separate scopes. FBi Buildings published an approximate $4,500 standard 12-by-12-foot stall-system benchmark in 2023; 20 gives $90,000 for that limited scope, not a completed barn or a 2026 Carolina installed bid. Barn sizing must include aisles, wash and tack areas, feed, staff space, ventilation and circulation. [S01, S02]

The base prototype separates shell/foundation/erection, stall systems, arena construction, non-arena grading, utilities, guest infrastructure and pasture. If a contractor's turnkey price already contains an item, remove the duplicate line rather than adding both. Hard-cost scope includes a 7,200-square-foot shell allowance at $65/square foot, with stall fit-out separate. These are placeholders, not a contractor quote. Ground works, fire protection, electrical and public-occupancy requirements can materially change the design.

Arena playing dimensions are a reference, not a complete site plan or cost quantity. Safety margins, access, drainage, spectator separation, paddocks, storage, turning areas and horse welfare all require additional space. The prototype is an outdoor arena program, not a covered arena or a full-size irrigated grass field. [S04, S18]

### Wood, steel and hybrid paddocks

Fence quantities must be measured as unique linear feet from a paddock plan; shared fence lines are counted once. Acreage alone is not enough. For perspective only, a square 20-acre perimeter is about 3,734 feet before internal paddocks, lanes and gates; an irregular parcel may need far more. Terrain, post spacing, rail count, coatings, safety detailing, corners and installation access matter.

The model defaults to 5,000 unique linear feet. Working allowances are $14/ft wood, $28/ft steel and a hybrid weighted 75% wood/25% steel. Gates, hardware and paddock water are a separate allowance. A Zareba guide gives a historic/undated $5–10/ft installed wood-fence range; it is context, not a contemporary quote supporting the entire model band. No local installed steel or hybrid quote has been verified. [S03]

Wood warrants an explicit inspection/replacement/painting plan; steel needs an engineered horse-safe specification, finish and inspection regime. Material selection is not merely price. Obtain three like-for-like installed scopes with taxes, freight, clearing, gates and maintenance exclusions identified.

## Paid staffing and operating responsibilities

The base program budgets a general manager/founder replacement, a head trainer/instructor and two groom full-time equivalents, plus a 22% employer burden allowance. This is a cash-cost model, not a plan to rely on unpaid founder labor. The default skilled-groom wage is $22/hour; the historical 2023 Charlotte BLS animal-worker mean of $16.12/hour is only context, not a current specialized hiring quote. [S19]

Convert those equivalents into an actual seven-day schedule covering feeding, turnout, stall cleaning, conditioning, lessons, event setup, leave, sickness and emergencies. Two full-time grooms do not by themselves prove that every shift is covered. A trainer selling lessons also cannot simultaneously supervise every event, train every horse and perform all administration. Staff housing, if required, is not specifically priced.

Boarded horses have a separate per-occupied-stall direct cost allowance. Confirm whether feed, bedding, turnout, blanketing, administering medication, vet/farrier holding and exercise are included in each boarding contract. Customer-paid veterinary/farrier costs are not included in the prototype boarding package. More boarders can require more staff and land even before all stalls are sold.

## NC and SC property taxes: value and permitted classification, not acres alone

**North Carolina:** annual tax is taxable assessed value divided by 100, multiplied by the applicable combined rate, plus applicable fees. Mecklenburg's FY2027 county rate is $0.4927 per $100: $4,927 per $1 million of taxable value for the county component only. Municipality, district and fees must be added where applicable. Do not apply Mecklenburg's rate to an SC or another NC-county property. [S08]

NC amended agricultural land's definition to include horse boarding effective for tax years beginning on or after July 1, 2022. Older guides excluding all boarding are not current on that point. Present-use-value classification is nevertheless not automatic: production acreage, gross income, ownership, sound management and other statutory requirements/exception provisions require review. The general agricultural criteria include 10 acres in actual production and average annual gross income of $1,000 over the preceding three years. Guest/event improvements and nonqualifying uses may remain taxed at ordinary value. [S05–S07]

**South Carolina:** annual tax generally equals applicable value × assessment ratio × millage / 1,000, plus fees. Ordinary nonresidential real property commonly uses 6%; qualifying agricultural property uses the applicable agricultural value and 4% or 6% ratio depending on ownership/classification. A homeowner's 4% legal-residence rate is not the default for a commercial club. Use the actual parcel district millage. A hypothetical $1 million value × 6% × 300 mills would be $18,000, but 300 is only an arithmetic example—not a verified rate for any candidate. [S09, S10]

The current SC statute provides rollback exposure for the change-in-use year and the three immediately preceding qualifying tax years, subject to its provisions. A legacy York application reviewed during this research states five prior years; do not copy that obsolete wording into an investor model. The statute is the controlling research reference, with assessor/counsel confirmation before acquisition. [S10]

The site editor has separate ordinary and qualifying agricultural values/ratios. These must not overlap. Current raw-land bills are not reliable forecasts of the post-build bill. Check mixed uses, improvements, district charges, rollback liability, reassessment timing and business personal-property tax. Tax agricultural classification does not establish zoning permission for public events.

## Revenue design and unit economics

The purpose of experience-center programming is to create contribution from customers who will not become regular polo players. The current model tests player dues, spectator tickets, introductory rider-sessions, boarding, corporate experiences, private/partner events and sponsorship. It does not assume a restaurant, hotel, full wedding complex or unlimited event calendar.

Each offer needs a defined buyer, capacity, price, direct cost, booking lead time, sales channel, repeat rate and cancellation policy. A corporate package may include instruction, an unmounted demonstration, hospitality and a match-viewing component; the actual content and insurance must be validated before sale. Public instruction at Triangle Area Polo provides a relevant format reference, but does not establish Charlotte's demand or pricing. [S17]

Contribution is sales less direct delivery costs and payment fees. Fixed operating costs are subtracted next, then property taxes, term debt service and a replacement/retirement cash reserve. The model exposes the gap between polo-only contribution and the proposed full facility burden. It also reverse-solves additional corporate events needed at the current contribution, and warns when those events exceed the available calendar. That reverse calculation is a diagnostic, not a sales forecast.

Validate the proposed model with paid pilots on a partner or leased site before large permanent commitments. Measure deposits, completed rider-sessions, rebooking, actual staff hours, delivery costs, cancellation losses and repeat attendance. Test corporate purchase intent with named buyers and written scopes, not a survey of general interest. Sponsorship remains uncommitted until contracted. Track gross booked amounts separately from collected cash and refundable deposits.

## Financial-model mechanics and boundaries

A five-operating-year forecast ramps requested demand at 45%, 70%, 90%, 100% and 100%, with the same capacity checks in every year. Fixed costs are not artificially ramped away. Prices and costs escalate independently. Monthly Year 1 liquidity spreads recurring dues/boarding contribution evenly and other contribution through an explicitly illustrative seasonal profile; it is not observed seasonality or a booking ledger.

Term debt uses a monthly amortizing payment formula, supports zero interest, and ends after the selected term. The cash coverage view is after the replacement allowance. Construction carry is a separate placeholder; there is no detailed draw schedule, floating-rate forecast or lender commitment. Land is a capital use, not an operating expense; horse purchase is separate from annual care. Replacement funding is cash reserved, not depreciation.

This is a pre-tax feasibility and cash-planning model, not yet a fully integrated balance sheet, tax depreciation model, partnership distribution waterfall, valuation, IRR model or formal lender underwriting package. Income taxes, debt closing details, working-capital deposit timing and a construction-month draw model need the final ownership/financing design. Sales are entered net of sales taxes; purchasing allowances must be converted to actual tax-inclusive delivered prices. No claim is made that all modeled allowances will cover every site-specific obligation.

## Unpriced or quote-dependent exposures to resolve

- Written commercial-use approvals; special-use hearings; lighting/noise hours; spectator capacity; fire access and water; public-occupancy, accessibility and restroom requirements; food/alcohol licenses and activity-specific insurance.
- Topography, soil bearing/rock, erosion and stormwater design, floodplain/wetlands, offsite road/utility extensions, entrance permits, trailer turning, septic expansion and water reliability. These are diligence items, not assertions that a particular permit applies to every site.
- General/property/equine liability, care/custody/control, workers' compensation, instructor/professional cover, event/liquor cover where applicable, umbrella limits, vehicle/trailer cover, deductibles and horse mortality/major medical where appropriate. Avoid double-counting premiums and medical provisions.
- Biosecurity, isolation, safe turnout, forage storage/fire risk, manure and fly control, emergency transport, severe-weather shutdown, refunds, backup staff and replacement horses. Horse welfare is a hard operating limit, not a cost optimization variable.
- Arena grooming and footing renewal, paddock repairs, mowing and equipment fuel/service, tack and helmet inspection/replacement, security, software, bookkeeping, card fees, marketing acquisition costs, staff housing if required, payroll compliance and retirement obligations.
- Land survey/title/legal, lenders' fees, holding costs, property/business personal taxes, rollback, escrow and insurance renewals, post-construction value changes, and liquidity to absorb a weak launch. Do not treat construction contingency as the operating-loss reserve.

## Three-site business-plan template and decision gates

For each property, complete the same market/catchment, layout, capital, annual operating, revenue/capacity, tax, financing and risk analysis. Record an owner for each outstanding item and dated supporting evidence. A checklist tick is user-recorded progress, not independent verification.

### Parcel / title

Address, parcel IDs, owner, asking price, acreage, deed, easements, covenants, survey and a broker availability check.

### Corridor / drive

Actual gate-to-Uptown route along I-77 south, intended weekday/weekend departure times, traffic range and dated route evidence. Maximum target: 45 minutes.

### Usable layout

Topographic survey, contiguous usable flat footprint, slope, soils, drainage, floodplain/wetlands, arena, paddocks and optional grass format drawn to scale.

### Permitted business uses

Written county/planning determination for commercial polo instruction, boarding, spectators, hospitality, events, food/alcohol and any noise/light limits. Agricultural tax status is not zoning approval.

### Infrastructure / access

Water flow and quality, electric upgrade, septic capacity for guests, accessible restrooms/parking, fire access/water, trailer turns and highway-entrance permission.

### Written cost scopes

Comparable installed bids for shell, fit-out, arena, three fence materials, grading, utilities and guest facilities. Identify exclusions, taxes, freight, escalation, permit fees and contingency.

### Post-development tax review

County and district, tax year, values split by qualifying use, current bill, post-build taxable value, rollback and business personal-property tax; CPA/assessor sign-off.

### Horse / staffing / insurance

Named trainer, vet and farrier; approved ration/workload/turnout plan; 365-day coverage; suitable purchase string; written underwriting for every proposed activity.

### Commercial validation

Paid pilots, repeat visits, member deposits, corporate letters of intent, event conversion, willingness to pay and actual contribution after labor/vendor costs.

### Funding / go-no-go

Lender term sheet, development schedule, contingency, downside liquidity, runway, ownership/governance and clear separation of club funds, charity funds and investor distributions.

## Phased execution proposal

**Phase 0 — validate demand and delivery:** Obtain a trainer-led operating plan and run paid small-scale experiences through an appropriate partner facility. Reconcile actual contribution and customer repeat behavior; do not acquire horses or land merely because the deck looks complete.

**Phase 1 — property feasibility:** Enter all three sites, inspect topography and improvements, validate route timing, obtain written use/tax guidance and comparable installed scopes. Model reuse credits only for genuinely serviceable assets; do not subtract both a discounted purchase price and a fictitious replacement value.

**Phase 2 — minimum viable opening:** Design the smallest permitted, properly insured and adequately staffed program meeting the owner's 20-stall/10-horse brief, with named funding for downside losses. A leased or existing equestrian facility is a scenario worth testing, not an assumed solution. A lease needs its own tenure, rent-escalation, improvement-ownership and exit analysis.

**Phase 3 — expansion only after evidence:** Add guest infrastructure, more horses or larger events when utilization, unit contribution, welfare, approvals and funded cash flow justify them. A covered arena or full grass field is a separate capital decision, not hidden in the outdoor-arena base case.
